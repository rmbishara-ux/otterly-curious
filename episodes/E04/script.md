# E04: Pip's Tippy Boat
big_question: Why do boats float?
value: Trying again
demo: A foil boat and a foil ball in a bathtub or sink; the wide boat floats and the small ball sinks.
book_title: Pip's Tippy Boat
bean_word: Try!
science: A wide, light boat shape pushes a lot of water aside, and the water pushes back up, so it floats. A small, heavy lump pushes only a little water aside, so it sinks.
demo_steps: Fill a sink or bathtub with water. Take two same-size pieces of foil. Squeeze one into a tight ball. Fold the other into a wide, flat boat with edges. Gently set both on the water. Watch the ball sink and the boat float. Then add a few small stones to the boat and see how many it can carry.
audit_note: Both pieces are the same foil, so the only difference is the shape; that is true and easy to see. Saying the wide shape pushes the water aside, and the water pushes back, is a kid-level version of buoyancy. It skips weight-versus-volume math. A heavy, loaded boat can still tip or sink if it is lopsided, which Pip learns in his first try.
