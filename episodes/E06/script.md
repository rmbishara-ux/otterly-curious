# E06: The Night Gus Told the Truth
big_question: Why does the lighthouse blink?
value: Honesty
demo: A flashlight inside a paper cup with a small hole, turned slowly in a dark room so the dot sweeps across the wall and seems to blink.
book_title: The Night Gus Told the Truth
bean_word: Truth!
science: Light travels in straight lines. A lighthouse lamp stays on, but it slowly turns, so its beam sweeps past you and away again. That looks like blinking, and it helps boats find the way home.
demo_steps: Ask a grown-up to help. Have the grown-up poke one small hole in the bottom of a paper cup. Put a flashlight inside the cup so its light shines out through the hole. Make the room dark. Aim at the wall, never at anyone's eyes. Turn slowly in a circle. The dot sweeps across the wall, then it is gone, then it comes back.
audit_note: The real lighthouse lamp has a big lens, but a cup with a hole makes a small beam the same way, and the script calls it a pretend lighthouse out loud. The lamp never really switches off; it is the turning beam that makes the blink, which is exactly what the demo shows. Real lighthouses also use light patterns to tell boats which lighthouse they see, which is left out as too much for age 3 to 7.
