# E09: Ollie and the Sky Travelers
big_question: How do birds know where to go?
value: Asking for help
demo: A paper arrow compass that points toward the sunny window, then following a drawn map around the room.
book_title: Gus Gets Lost
bean_word: Help!
science: Birds use the sun, the stars, and landmarks like the coastline to find their way, and many travel together.
demo_steps: Draw an arrow on a piece of paper and lay it flat on a table. Turn it so it points at the sunny window. Now walk to a new spot, lay the arrow down, and turn it to the window again. Next, draw a simple map of your room with a few landmarks, like the bed, the door, and the chair. Follow the drawn path with your finger, then walk it with a friend.
audit_note: The paper arrow is a pretend compass, and the script says "pretend" out loud. It shows that the sun stays in one place in the sky for a long while, so it can be a guide. Real birds also keep track of how the sun moves during the day, and they use the stars at night. Some birds also sense the Earth's magnetism, but the script leaves that out because kids cannot try it at home. The map shows how landmarks like a coastline help a bird see where it is. Many birds travel in groups and the older ones know the way, which is true for some kinds of birds, and the script keeps it simple and says "many."
